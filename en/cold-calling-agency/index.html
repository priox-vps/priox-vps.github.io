Where the line runs

Cold calling agency or call centre: the difference starts before the call

A call centre organises reach. That can be right for service or simple enquiries. In B2B appointment setting for offerings that need explaining, however, the work sits inside the conversation: understand the trigger, place the roles, take up questions and, after the second objection, not move on to the next record. That takes people with sales practice of their own.

At DEALMAKERS the calls are made by fixed sales partners from our own network. They have years of their own B2B sales experience. There are no staff trained up at short notice, no dialling machine and no target for calls per hour. Anyone only looking for the next tick does not hear the difference between a final no and one that is still open.

We do not call down a list, we call on a trigger.

Trigger before list

Why the phone still works in B2B

If the word „trigger“ means nothing to you in this context: the usual approach is to call a list of target clients from top to bottom – who you reach is then decided by the order of the file. We turn that around and wait for an event that becomes publicly visible at the company you want, a job advert for instance or a new site. Only then do we call. The page on Signal Based Selling sets this out in full.

A phone call clarifies in a few sentences whether a topic is relevant now, later or not at all. That does not work through volume or persistence, but when the opening connects a public event with the offering in a way anyone can follow.

Which company is due right now is reported by our own central sales intelligence system: it continuously evaluates millions of sources carrying relevant data and recognises more than 40 kinds of trigger – and if your market needs 100 of them, it will be 100. How that layer works is shown by PrioX; we built the software ourselves. The approach behind it is called Signal Based Selling.

The first sentence names only this single, openly visible event, never a pattern we have observed. For example: „Last week you advertised two sales roles in Vienna. Who owns the decision at your end on how the first conversations there come about?“ Or: „Your website has been announcing the new product line since Monday. Is the pipeline for it already a topic with you?“ Both sentences stay with the trigger and do not overlay it with an interpretation.

The craft

The structure of a call in five phases

We stay with the example above – the advertised sales role for the new site. The sentences in the cards below serve only to make the point clear; in your case there is a different trigger, a different role and a different question. The sequence stays, the content is written anew for your market.

A good first conversation needs no monologue, it needs a framework. The first twenty seconds create permission, not pressure to close. Only once role and trigger are confirmed or corrected does a question about a meeting make sense.

  1. 1. Name the trigger: 0:00–0:20

    The sentence is short and verifiable: „You have advertised the lead role for the new site.“ Then comes the question of who is responsible. No hidden display of research, no assessment of the company.

  2. 2. Get permission: 0:20–0:40

    „Have I caught you for two short questions on the sales side?“ That gives the person a way out. Whoever does not offer it rarely gets usable answers.

  3. 3. Clarify the situation: 0:40–1:30

    One open question is enough: „How are you currently winning first meetings for the new region?“ Then listen. The pause after the question is part of the conversation, not a mistake.

  4. 4. Understand the objection: 1:30–3:00

    „No need“ can mean: wrong moment, wrong role or a genuine refusal. Experience shows in asking first instead of reading out the next sentence of the script.

  5. 5. Agree the next step: to 4:00

    Where topic and role fit, the meeting becomes concrete: participants, duration, question and time. Where they do not, a later window or a clean close is recorded.

Voice, pace, pause

A calm voice is no substitute for substance, but it makes substance audible. People with sales experience vary pace and wording instead of filling every pause with an argument.

This is a different brief from winning new clients as a complete service: here it becomes particularly visible whether the person on the phone can lead on the substance. An experienced caller answers the second objection with a question or an honest close. A script often only supplies the next pre-written sentence.

Nor do we necessarily stop at the meeting. On request we carry the cycle further: accompany the proposal, follow up, negotiate and close within the price range you release. For deep technical questions we bring in your people. Only the care of your existing clients is something we do not take on – that is account management and lives on history and a relationship that has grown.

No trick needed

Why the gatekeeper rarely comes up with us

Half the trade trains how to get past reception. We ask the question before that: why do we end up there at all? As a rule we know who is responsible and what is currently occupying that person, and we have the direct line or the mobile number. The gatekeeper is then simply not in play – there is nothing to get around.

And where somebody does stand in between, it is not a trick that decides but the trigger. „It is about the sales role for Vienna that has been advertised since Monday“ is something completely different for an assistant from „I have a question about sales“. A specific, verifiable reason gives them a basis to decide – and that same reason later carries the meeting. No false name, no invented call back: whoever needs that has no trigger.

The voice decides too – in the first three seconds

Before the person opposite has assessed your first sentence on its content, they have long since filed the caller away. That runs through the pitch at the end of the sentence. If it goes up, every sentence sounds like a request for permission – the typical sound of the run-of-the-mill seller who is not sure whether they may interrupt. We deliberately work with the falling pitch, the Downwards Inflection: the sentence ends lower than it began. That is the tone in which people give information instead of asking for something.

Chris Voss, for years a lead negotiator at the FBI, calls the calm, warm, downward variant the „late-night FM DJ voice“: slow, warm, falling. It takes pace out of the conversation instead of winding it up – and a calm counterpart is the condition for being listened to at all. Three places make the difference here: giving your own name, the trigger sentence and the question about a meeting. The question about a meeting above all. That cannot be handed out as a script, somebody has to be able to do it – and it is one of the reasons why no temporary staff sit on the phone with us.

The lever before the call

We call when somebody is really at their desk

The most common reason a call fails is not the person at the other end but the moment. Whoever dials a list from top to bottom catches a large part of their target group in the middle of a meeting. So we do not guess when somebody is free – we check it. We use tools that read calendar availability, including calendars that are not our own, and place the call deliberately in an open window.

Together with the direct line or the mobile number, that is the real lever behind our call-to-connect rate: whoever is not sitting in a meeting and whose phone rings directly will as a rule pick up. Whether the conversation turns into a meeting is then decided by the trigger – but first somebody has to answer at all. What that means in figures is in the next section.

The craft behind it

Objection handling that does not sound like objection handling

What you know: the caller hears „no need“ and adds a counter-argument learned by heart. That is the standard school – and it fails precisely because the person opposite recognises it within two seconds. For us an objection is not a statement that has to be refuted but the only real information we get at that moment. Three sentences carry the way we lead a conversation:

Calm first, then talk

As long as somebody is in defensive mode they hear no arguments – they are looking for the exit. The first task on the phone is not persuasion but a calm counterpart. Everything else comes after that or not at all.

Pressure never comes from us

We do not create urgency, we make the existing urgency visible. The reason to act lies in their situation, not in our offering. Whoever has grasped that never has to push – and is therefore never brushed off.

Rapport is a precondition

Nobody buys from someone they experience as an opponent. That is why we practically never contradict on the phone. Agreeing and still making progress is a technique, not a character trait.

The difference in figures

Standard agency and DEALMAKERS: same day, different figures

A classic cold calling agency works through a list: call the switchboard, convince the gatekeeper, recite the pitch, next number. That way it takes 60 to 100 dial attempts to arrive at 8 to 20 conversations and one to three meetings a day. That is the honest figure for the trade – and the reason why cold calling is regarded as a pure numbers game.

We turn three screws before a single number is dialled: we only call when the person is available at that moment. We have the direct line or the mobile number, so there is no switchboard in between. And we have a trigger that carries the meeting by itself. Where all three come together we reach up to 60 per cent of the people called – and 40 per cent of those conversations turn into a meeting.

Transparency

How we secure quality

Cold calling must not disappear into a report. Quality is measured on the conversation and on the handover, not only on dial attempts. At the start, and whenever needed, we listen in on calls, check notes and discuss wording, objections, availability and meeting quality every week.

Notes record the trigger, the role, the objection, the meeting status and the next step. That lets your sales team pick up from there. It prevents calendar slots without context.

Many teams already have lead scoring in their CRM, in HubSpot or in a spreadsheet. That stays as it is. We sharpen it: the fit describes whether a company is suitable in principle; the trigger describes whether something has just happened. Live signals from public sources have time windows of their own, because events age. For the top of the list, deep research follows, so that a first sentence emerges and not just a number. That also makes suitable companies visible that are not yet in the CRM. How that layer works is described by lead scoring at PrioX; we built the software ourselves and use it ourselves.

Frequent questions

Questions about the cold calling agency

What does cold calling cost per meeting?

A price per meeting only makes sense once meeting quality, target group and follow-up work have been agreed. With B2B offerings that need explaining, there is research, several dial attempts, call notes and a meeting confirmation. That is why we agree the criteria and the scope of work first; a flat price per meeting without that basis is not something we consider reliable.

How many calls does one meeting take?

As an order of magnitude we reckon with 30 to 60 dial attempts for one qualified meeting when the target group fits. A dial attempt is not a conversation: several attempts can concern the same person. How many are needed depends on availability, trigger, offering and on whether the meeting is agreed with the right role.

When is the best time for B2B calls?

There is no time of day that holds everywhere. Our experience often points to windows in the morning after the day has started and in the later afternoon, when internal meetings ease off. What matters is sector, role and time zone. We document availability per target group and adjust calling times to it instead of following a blanket rule.

Do you work with scripts?

No, we work with an agreed call framework, not with a word-for-word script. Trigger, objective, permitted statements, questions and meeting criteria are prepared. The actual response to questions, pauses and the second objection is led by the person on the phone. Otherwise a call sounds like a text module and ends accordingly early.

Do you call under our name?

We settle that clearly before we start. Depending on the project we speak openly as the sales agency you have engaged, or in the agreed name of your company. In both cases name, role and trigger stay correct. We do not use false names and do not create the impression of an employment that does not exist.

Is cold calling allowed in B2B?

Towards companies, telephone advertising can be permissible under the conditions of the German Act against Unfair Competition, in particular where presumed consent may be considered because of a factual link to the business of the person called. Whether that applies in the individual case depends on the offering, the contact and the approach. We call in a business context only, without recorded announcements, automatic dialling systems or false names. This is guidance, not legal advice; in case of doubt have the specific case checked legally.

How do you get past the gatekeeper?

Often we do not need to: we know the person in charge and their current topic before the call, and we frequently have the direct line or the email address. Where the route runs through reception, we state the trigger, our own role and the person we want to speak to, give enough context for a decision and respect a no.

What counts as a qualified meeting?

A qualified meeting is a firmly agreed conversation with a role that owns the topic or decides on it alongside others. There is a comprehensible trigger, a topic that has been discussed and a clear next step. A calendar slot without interest, without context or with the wrong person does not count as a qualified meeting for us.

Do we receive call notes?

Yes. For every relevant conversation we record the trigger, the contact, the reaction, objections, the agreed next step and the meeting status. The note is meant to help your sales team, not to simulate a long transcript. The weekly review additionally shows you which objections and which time windows keep recurring.

How many meetings per week are realistic?

For a focused B2B target group, one to three qualified meetings per working day is a usable order of magnitude, so five to fifteen per week with calling running continuously. That is not a promise. With narrow target groups, complex offerings or poor availability the figure is lower. We only judge the number once the objective and the call criteria are set.

What sets you apart from a call centre?

At DEALMAKERS the calls are led by people with years of their own B2B sales experience. They work as fixed partners from our own network, not in shift operation and not to a quantity per hour. Before the call there is a specific, publicly visible trigger. That leaves room for questions on the substance, for pauses and for the second objection.

Do you also handle the meeting confirmation?

Yes, where it is part of the agreed process. The meeting is then confirmed with time, participants, topic and the context agreed in the call. Cancellations or postponements are documented as such. Final responsibility for running the specialist conversation stays with your team, which can represent the solution itself.
